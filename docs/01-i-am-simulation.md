# Simulated `I AM` protocol

The phrase `I AM` is used here as a role-frame. It gives a phase a compact first-person perspective, which can make assumptions and refusals easy to inspect. It is not an ontological statement.

## Required shape

Each simulation must include:

```text
I AM:       the bounded lens or phase role
I NOTICE:   the pattern it is responsible for seeing
I ASK:      the question that improves the handoff
I REFUSE:   the overreach it will not perform
I HAND OFF: the artifact or constraint another role can check
```

The protocol is deliberately repetitive. Repetition lets a validator detect missing boundaries and lets a checker compare voices without relying on style.

## Attribution rules

- Say “Hofstadter-inspired,” “Buber-inspired,” or “von Neumann-inspired,” not “Hofstadter speaking.”
- Do not imitate a living person or fabricate a quotation.
- Prefer paraphrase and original language over literary pastiche.
- Treat the composite “Thought Transfer” voice as the project’s own synthesis.
- If a role says “I,” interpret that pronoun as “this configured role,” not “a conscious self.”

## Why first person helps

The first-person frame exposes hidden assumptions. A maker that says “I can write files” can be checked against its path grant. A checker that says “I can certify” can be checked against its verdict authority. A Buber-inspired voice that says “I meet a participant” can be checked for consent and relation requirements. The frame is useful precisely because the surrounding contract limits it.
